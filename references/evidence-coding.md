# Evidence Coding

Use this before writing any slide. The goal is to keep observation, inference, and wishful thinking apart.

## Labels

| Label | Meaning | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `[course pXX]` | Explicit instructor requirement or course content | Cite the deck page. |
| `[group-confirmed]` | The group actually decided it | Only after a real decision. |
| `[observed]` | You can see the behaviour in a post, photo, video, price page, or rule | Quote or describe what is visible. |
| `[self-report]` | The author says they do/want it, but you did not see it | Do not call it behaviour. |
| `[inferred]` | Your interpretation | Name the inference. |
| `[assumption]` | No evidence yet | Mark it and test it. |
| `[not found]` | You searched and did not find it | Do not fill the cell with a guess. |

## Evidence transfer errors

These are the most common ways a deck lies without inventing a number:

1. **Relationship transfer:** stranger-buddy posts used to prove a close-friend occasion, or family posts used to prove a two-friend occasion.
2. **Behaviour transfer:** a self-report ("I would share food") used as an observed behaviour.
3. **Scope transfer:** one park, one day, or one post used to claim a city-wide or universal rule.
4. **Feature transfer:** a competitor's missing feature treated as user demand.
5. **Mention transfer:** "the sun was strong" treated as a decision-changing constraint, even though nobody changed time, place, or gear.
6. **Sample transfer:** friends and design students used as a representative sample.

## What a source can support

Match the claim to the source type. A source that supports one sentence often cannot support the next:

| Source | Can support | Cannot support |
|---|---|---|
| Price / product page | The substitute exists; a price anchor; listed features | Demand, willingness to pay, actual use |
| Participatory design workshop | Participants' expectations, vocabulary, design directions | Effect, adoption, behaviour change |
| Lab experiment | A measured effect in that task and sample | Real-world retention or a different outcome |
| Public post with photos | A visible setup or one occasion | Frequency, representativeness, city-wide rule |
| Self-report / testimonial | How someone describes the problem | What they actually do or will pay |
| Meta-analysis | An average effect with heterogeneity | A specific product's effect or a causal claim outside the included studies |

When a source only proves existence, write "exists" or "is listed", not "there is demand" or "users will pay".

## Metric rules

- Prefer a **rate** over a raw count: `events ÷ eligible opportunities`.
- Report both the raw count and the rate; a busy week can inflate a count and make an intervention look effective.
- If the baseline is zero, relative lift `(B − A) / A` is undefined. Report the **percentage-point difference** instead.
- Pre-register the comparison, metric, and calculation before the test; do not choose the metric after seeing the result.

## Mention vs decision-changing constraint

A constraint counts only when it changes a choice:

- changed time (avoid noon);
- changed place (choose a shaded park);
- changed spot (move under a tree);
- changed object (bring umbrella, mat, canopy);
- changed behaviour (cancel, move indoors, order delivery).

A post that only says "the sun was strong" is a mention, not a constraint.

## Quote rules

- Use quotation marks only for a verified verbatim line.
- If you only have a paraphrase, write `转述摘要` plus source and date; never put it in quotation marks.
- If you cannot verify the original sentence, write `未能核实原句`.
- Do not merge two comments into one "quote".

## Absolute claims

Avoid:

- "没有人做"
- "所有人都需要"
- "十点后一定没位置"
- "这个品类没有竞品"

Use:

- "在公开检索到的产品里，没有看到……；检索范围有限"
- "在这篇帖子里，作者提到……；不能推出全城"
- "在我们找到的 3 个竞品里……"

## Counterexample rule

Every evidence page should include at least one counterexample or negative case:

- someone who prefers the opposite (sun vs shade);
- someone who solved it with a commodity substitute;
- someone who does not have the problem;
- a failed or abandoned product in the category.

A page with only supporting evidence is a prosecution brief, not research.
