# Grill Protocol

Use this when a concept, positioning, or deck must survive adversarial review. The purpose is not to sound tough; it is to force a decision.

Start with the strongest disconfirming question. The [question bank](question-bank.md) supplies prompts for particular claims and sources.

## Roles

One person can wear all hats, but write the hat down so the voices do not blur.

| Role | Job |
|---|---|
| Advocate | States the claim, evidence, and the decision requested. |
| Critic | Attacks the weakest link, not every link. One main attack per round. |
| Evidence Clerk | Checks provenance, sample bias, labels, counterexamples, and evidence transfer. |
| Decider | Writes continue / narrow / pivot / kill, with the reason and next evidence. |

## Three grills

### 1. Claim grill

Before research or slides. The claim must be falsifiable.

Ask:

- Who exactly, in what scene, how often?
- What is the existing substitute, including doing nothing?
- What measurable loss does it cause?
- What mechanism does the concept use?
- What metric and threshold would prove it?
- What result would make the claim false?

If the claim cannot be filled with a behaviour and a number, it is an idea, not a hypothesis.

### 2. Evidence grill

After public research or observation.

Ask:

- Where did this come from? URL, date, author, context.
- Is it observation, self-report, inference, or assumption?
- Is the sample from the same relationship context?
- What is the strongest counterexample?
- Does the quote exist verbatim, or is it a paraphrase?
- Does the evidence show a decision-changing constraint, or just a mention?

### 3. Deck grill

Before submission.

Ask:

- What question does this page answer?
- Which sentence is a claim, which is evidence, which is decoration?
- Is the Who a behaviour or a demographic?
- Is the What a promise or a product description?
- Where is the weakest touchpoint?
- Which page would survive if the logo were removed?
- What is still unverified, and is it labelled?

## Rounds

Use the rounds that apply to the material under review. A grill-only request can start from a claim alone. Review a deck when one exists.

### Round 1 — Claim

- Advocate reads the one-page claim.
- Critic asks the strongest disconfirming question.
- Evidence Clerk labels every claim.
- Decider chooses: continue to evidence, narrow, pivot, or kill.

### Round 2 — Evidence

- Advocate presents the evidence table.
- Critic attacks sample, transfer, counterexamples, and missing behaviour.
- Evidence Clerk separates observation from inference.
- Decider chooses: continue to position, narrow, pivot, or kill.

### Round 3 — Deck

- Advocate presents the 8-page outline.
- Critic attacks the weakest page and the strongest alternative explanation.
- Decider chooses: continue to production, cut pages, or return to position.

Stop after three rounds. Do not add new evidence after round 3; decide with what exists and write the next evidence request.

## Five rules

1. **No claim, no grill.** Write the falsifiable sentence first.
2. **Every question cites a source.** A page, a URL, a quote, a price, a rule, or a slide.
3. **Allow "I don't know".** Downgrade it to `[assumption]`; do not decorate it.
4. **Every round ends with a decision.** Continue / narrow / pivot / kill.
5. **Require a deletion.** At least one sentence must be deleted or rewritten per grill.

## Good grill vs theatre

A grill is valuable only if at least one of these happens:

- a sentence is deleted;
- a claim is narrowed;
- a source is downgraded;
- a kill criterion is written;
- a direction is killed or pivoted.

If the debate was sharp but nothing changed, it was theatre.

## Decision record

Use the [decision-log template](../templates/decision-log.md) as the input for the project's grill record. Record the sentence to delete and the decision to change. For a single grill, deliver one page covering the verdict, strongest challenge, evidence status, deleted sentence, decision, kill criteria, and minimum next evidence. Include a reframe when it changes the decision. Seek real observations, measurements, photos, screenshots, or test results to resolve the weakest link.

The [output paths](outputs.md) specify where the completed record belongs for each mode.

## Tone

- Attack the claim, not the person.
- Ask for the smallest piece of evidence that would change the decision.
- Do not accept adjectives as evidence.
- Do not rescue a dead concept because the render looks good.
- If the cheapest substitute wins, say so and kill it.
